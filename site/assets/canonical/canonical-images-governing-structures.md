# Canonical Location Photographs and Governing Structures

A review of the seven canonical Northing locations revealed that several placeholder concepts used during prompt-generator development had drifted from the actual location corpus. The placeholder locations "Old Road Trace," "Pond Edge," and "Hemlock Ravine" were replaced by the finalized locations Forest Crossing, Beaver Meadow, and Headwater Spring. This required reevaluation of what each location's canonical photograph should communicate.

A key insight emerged during image selection: the strongest canonical photographs were not the most beautiful or atmospheric. They were the images that most clearly communicated the governing structure of the place. Canonical assets function primarily as continuity anchors for future imagery, artifacts, and video generation.

The finalized governing structures are:

- TC-001 Tributary Confluence Wet Meadow → Convergence
- AO-001 Abandoned Hillside Orchard → Persistence
- FC-001 Forest Crossing → Passage
- BM-001 Beaver Meadow → Transformation
- SF-001 Stone Foundation Clearing → Reclamation
- HS-001 Headwater Spring → Emergence
- RO-001 Ridge Overlook → Orientation

An important conceptual shift occurred during the process. The locations are now understood primarily through relationships and processes rather than objects or scenery. Water begins, converges, reshapes, and is crossed. Human order persists and is reclaimed. The landscape becomes legible through orientation. This framework appears more consistent with the philosophical direction of Northing than earlier object-based descriptions.

The Beaver Meadow and Headwater Spring reviews demonstrated the value of this approach. Early candidate images inadvertently solved older placeholder concepts ("Pond Edge" and "Ravine & Hemlock Enclosure"). Once the prompts were rewritten around transformation and emergence, the resulting images aligned much more closely with the actual identities of the locations.

Current assessment: the seven-location canonical framework appears stable and internally coherent.
